## Routines

It's necessary to have consistent routines for job applications, skill improvement, and skill refreshment.

### Job applications

Regularly (daily) scan and save job postings and companies that interest me on LinkedIn, even if the job is outside of my expertise.
This results in a list of 50-60 jobs.

Every Monday, until the list of saved jobs is exhausted, I apply to the positions that I'm interested in.
I tailor different resumes for different jobs, highlighting the skills and experience most relevant to the job description.

I save significant time during the application process by using [Simplify](https://simplify.jobs/) to rapidly fill out repetitive application details.
This is not an ad.
I am not affiliated.
It's a tool that I found useful for my job hunt.

I apply to jobs using a specific email on a custom domain.
My gmail inbox is configured to tag and filter these emails, allowing me to easily track which applications I've sent.

### Skill improvement

Being good at I do involves staying up to date with what's happening in my field.
I maintain a general idea of what skills are required in my field of work primarily through reading technical books and papers.
Other sources include following engineers in my field on Twitter/LinkedIn, attending conferences, reading job postings or newsletters, and reflecting upon my past work experience.

I use this knowledge to pinpoint my weaknesses and develop personal projects to eliminate my weaknesses.
Personal projects range from simple to complex, from data engineering in my home lab to machine learning in the cloud.
During project development, I iterate quickly by explaining the project to others and refining the project based on their feedback.

I cannot explain in words how important personal projects are for skill improvement.
It's as close as you can get to real-world, practical experience.
Plus, it gives you so much to talk about during the interview process.

ChatGPT is another invaluable tool.
I use ChatGPT to explain concepts while reading books and provide guidance while developing projects.
It's not a silver bullet, but it guides me through the initial rough stages of learning - which is figuring out what questions to ask.

Grinding leetcode/hackerrank is **not** a good way to improve skills.
It's not practical.
It's simply a method of memorizing programming problems and solutions.

Skill improvement can be boiled down to: learn about what's possible, apply what you've just learned, and practice what you already know.
This routine is especially important when applying to positions that I want, but may not have all the necessary skills.

### Skill refreshment

When I don't use a skill on a regular basis - such as a programming language, domain knowledge, or tool - I forget the intricacies.
To keep my skills sharp, it is important for me to have a routine to refresh, review, and apply my skills.

To review high-level concepts, I regularly create and review Anki flashcards.
Flashcards allow me to steadily build and refresh conceptual knowledge from technical books and papers.

To review programming concepts, I do two things:

1. design (or review) small personal projects
2. complete leetcode/hackerrank problems

It's difficult to keep *every single* concept fresh in my mind.
I focus my review on concepts that are most relevant to my upcoming interviews, as found in the job posting's "requirements" section.
